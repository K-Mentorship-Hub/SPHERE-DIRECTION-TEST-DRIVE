# Quest 9 — MilTech & Dual-Use

**Sphere:** Bridge | **Time:** 2-3 hours | **Difficulty:** Intermediate

---

## Scenario

You're advising a drone startup that wants to sell to both civilian and defense markets. They need to understand the ethical, legal, and strategic implications of dual-use technology. Write a decision framework.

---

## Theory Bite

Dual-use technology can be used for **both beneficial and harmful purposes**. The core tension:

- Civilian applications (agriculture, search & rescue, infrastructure inspection)
- Defense applications (surveillance, reconnaissance, logistics)
- Ethical boundaries (what uses are you comfortable enabling?)
- Legal frameworks (export controls, ITAR, dual-use regulations)

Key truth: **the technology is neutral. The application is not.** Working in this space requires clear personal and organizational boundaries.

---

## Hands-On Task

### Part 1: Dual-Use Map (30 min)

Pick one technology (drones, AI vision, encryption, GPS). List:
- 3 civilian use cases
- 3 defense use cases
- 1 "red line" use case you'd refuse to support

### Part 2: Stakeholder Analysis (30 min)

Identify 4 stakeholders for a dual-use product:
- Customers (who buys it?)
- Regulators (who approves it?)
- Public (who is affected?)
- Employees (who builds it?)

What does each stakeholder care about? Where do their interests conflict?

### Part 3: Decision Framework (45 min)

Create a simple decision tree for the startup:
- When is a customer request acceptable?
- When should you say no?
- What questions should you always ask before selling?

### Part 4: Ethics Brief (30 min)

Write a 1-page brief: "Why We Will and Won't Do" — clearly stating the company's dual-use boundaries. Make it specific enough that a salesperson could use it to make a decision.

---

## Reflection Questions

- Were you comfortable navigating ethical ambiguity?
- Did the strategic/analytical thinking engage you?
- Could you work in an industry where your work has life-and-death implications?
