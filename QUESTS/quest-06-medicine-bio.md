# Quest 6 — Medicine / Biology

**Sphere:** S | **Time:** 2-3 hours | **Difficulty:** Beginner-friendly

---

## Scenario

You're a research assistant at a small biotech startup. Your first task: translate a complex scientific paper into something a non-scientist can understand — because the startup needs investors, and investors don't speak "PubMed."

---

## Theory Bite

Working at the intersection of tech and biology means **translating between worlds**. The daily reality:

- Reading dense scientific literature and extracting what matters
- Designing experiments or studies (rigor matters — lives depend on it)
- Communicating findings to non-scientists (doctors, investors, regulators)
- Navigating ethics, regulation, and evidence standards

Key truth: **in science, being wrong is expensive.** You need patience, precision, and comfort with uncertainty.

---

## Hands-On Task

### Part 1: Read a Paper (45 min)

Find one open-access paper on PubMed (search any topic that interests you). Read the abstract and introduction. Answer:
- What question was the study trying to answer?
- What did they find?
- What are the limitations?

### Part 2: Plain-Language Summary (30 min)

Rewrite the paper's conclusion in 3 sentences that a 12-year-old could understand. No jargon. No acronyms.

### Part 3: Evidence Ladder (30 min)

Rate the evidence quality of your paper on a simple scale:

| Level | Type |
|-------|------|
| 5 | Systematic review / meta-analysis |
| 4 | Randomized controlled trial |
| 3 | Cohort study |
| 2 | Case study |
| 1 | Expert opinion / anecdote |

Where does your paper fall? Why does this matter?

### Part 4: Problem Map (30 min)

Pick one health/biology problem you care about. Map it:

- **Problem:** What's wrong?
- **Actors:** Who is affected? Who could help?
- **Interventions:** What solutions exist? What's missing?
- **Tech opportunity:** Where could technology help?
