# Quest 5 — Design & UX

**Sphere:** Bridge | **Time:** 2-4 hours | **Difficulty:** Beginner-friendly

---

## Scenario

A local coffee shop wants a simple website. They don't need anything fancy — just a way for customers to see the menu, find the location, and order online. You're the designer. No code required — just thinking, sketching, and making it usable.

---

## Theory Bite

Design & UX is about **making things work for humans**. The core loop:

1. **Empathize** — understand who's using this and what they need
2. **Define** — what problem are we solving?
3. **Ideate** — sketch multiple solutions quickly
4. **Prototype** — make something testable (even on paper)
5. **Test** — watch someone try to use it

Key truth: **design is not about making things pretty. It's about making things clear.** A beautiful app that confuses users is bad design. An ugly app that everyone can use is good design.

Tools: paper & pen, Figma (free), Excalidraw, or any drawing tool

---

## Hands-On Task

### Part 1: User Research (20 min)

Interview one person (or imagine the interview) about ordering coffee:
- What information do they need before visiting a coffee shop?
- What frustrates them about coffee shop websites?
- Would they order online or just walk in?

Write 3 key insights from your research.

### Part 2: Information Architecture (30 min)

List all the content the coffee shop website needs:
- Menu (drinks, food, prices)
- Location & hours
- Online ordering
- About / story

Organize these into a **site map** — what goes on the homepage? What's a sub-page? What's most important?

### Part 3: Wireframe the Homepage (60 min)

Sketch the homepage layout. Include:
- Navigation
- Hero section (what's the first thing people see?)
- Menu highlights
- Location & hours
- Call to action (order online)

Use paper, Figma, or any tool. Don't worry about colors or fonts — focus on **where things go and why**.

### Part 4: Usability Test (30 min)

Show your wireframe to one person. Ask them:
- "Where would you click to see the full menu?"
- "How would you order a latte?"
- "What's the first thing you notice?"

Note where they hesitate or get confused. **Redesign that part.**

## Reflection Questions

- Did you enjoy thinking about how other people experience things?
- Was sketching fun or frustrating?
- Did you care more about the layout (UX) or the visual style (UI)?
- Could you handle the ambiguity of "there's no single right answer"?
